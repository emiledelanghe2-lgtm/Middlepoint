Arguing about money in a relationship: why it is rarely about money

Written by the Middlepoint team, October 20, 2026.

An argument about money quickly feels like an argument about numbers. Usually it is about security, freedom or recognition. Once you understand that, you talk about money differently.

Money means something different to everyone

For one person money is security: a buffer, no unexpected expenses. For another, money is freedom: enjoying the moment. A third sees it as recognition: what you spend on me shows that I matter. When these meanings collide, it is not the budget that clashes but the need behind it.

Start with the question beneath the number

Ask each other: 'What does money mean to you, and what are you afraid of?' Listen without defending yourself. You will often hear that the fear is much older than the relationship: growing up with little, a debt, a loss.

Make it concrete and safe

Schedule a regular money conversation, for example once a month, at a calm moment and not in the middle of an argument. Start with what is going well. Then talk about one topic at a time.

Find a structure that works for both of you

Many couples choose a joint account for fixed costs and an amount of their own per person that does not have to be justified. That gives both certainty and freedom. What works differs. What matters is that it is a shared choice.

When money is a sign of something bigger

If secrets, hidden debts or control are involved, it is no longer an ordinary argument. Then seek help from a therapist or a credit counseling service, for example a nonprofit agency in your area.

Please note. Is this about violence, threats or fear of the other person? Then an online conversation is not the right place. In the US you can call the National Domestic Violence Hotline for free at 1-800-799-7233, and in the UK the National Domestic Abuse Helpline at 0808 2000 247. In Flanders you can call 1712 for free. In case of immediate danger, call 911 (US), 999 (UK) or 112 (Europe).
